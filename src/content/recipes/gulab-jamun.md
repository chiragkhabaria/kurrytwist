# Introduction

Gulab Jamun is India's most beloved dessert—soft, spongy milk dumplings soaked in cardamom-scented sugar syrup. These golden beauties are a must-have at Indian weddings, festivals, and celebrations.

# Ingredients

## For the Syrup
- 2 cups sugar
- 2 cups water
- 4-5 cardamom pods, crushed
- 1 tsp rose water
- Few saffron strands (optional)
- 1 tsp lemon juice

## For the Dumplings
- 1 cup milk powder
- 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
- 1/4 tsp baking soda
- 2 tbsp ghee (melted)
- 4-5 tbsp milk (as needed)
- Pinch of cardamom powder

## For Frying
- Ghee or oil for deep frying

# Instructions

1. **Make the syrup**: Combine sugar and water in a pot. Bring to boil, stirring until sugar dissolves. Add crushed cardamom and saffron. Simmer for 5 minutes until slightly sticky. Add rose water and lemon juice. Turn off heat and keep warm.

2. **Prepare the dough**: In a bowl, mix milk powder, flour, baking soda, and cardamom powder. Add melted ghee and mix.

3. **Form the dough**: Gradually add milk, one tablespoon at a time, and gently knead into a soft, smooth dough. Don't over-knead. The dough should be soft but not sticky.

4. **Shape dumplings**: Divide dough into 20 small portions. Roll each into a smooth ball between your palms. Make sure there are no cracks. If too dry, dampen hands slightly.

5. **Heat oil**: Heat ghee or oil in a deep pan on low-medium heat. The oil should not be too hot—test with a small piece of dough. It should rise slowly.

6. **Fry the dumplings**: Gently slide 5-6 balls into the oil. Fry on low heat, continuously stirring gently to ensure even browning. This takes 8-10 minutes per batch.

7. **Achieve golden color**: The balls will puff up and turn golden brown. Remove when they reach a rich golden color.

8. **Soak in syrup**: Immediately transfer hot gulab jamuns to warm syrup. Let them soak for at least 2 hours before serving. They'll absorb the syrup and become soft.

9. **Serve**: Serve warm or at room temperature. Garnish with chopped pistachios or rose petals if desired.

# Tips

- **Dough consistency is key**: Too soft and they'll break; too hard and they'll be dense. Aim for soft, pliable dough.
- **No cracks**: Smooth balls without cracks are essential. Cracks will cause them to break while frying.
- **Low heat frying**: This is crucial! High heat will brown the outside but leave the inside raw.
- **Make ahead**: Gulab jamun taste better the next day after soaking overnight in syrup.
- **Storage**: Store in syrup at room temperature for 2-3 days or refrigerate for up to a week.
- **Reheating**: Microwave for 10-15 seconds to serve warm.
